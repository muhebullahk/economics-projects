SC
Eh Sayers · Ep. 26 · July 16, 2025
"A Tariff on Both Your Houses!"

Guy Gellatly, Chief Economic Advisor at Statistics Canada, explains the Canada–US tariff conflict: how US exports represent roughly 17% of Canadian GDP, which sectors (manufacturing, oil & gas, autos) are most exposed, and which indicators — employment, trade flows, investment, and pricing — to watch as the shock works through the economy.

Open Economy · Aggregate Demand
SC
Eh Sayers · Ep. 20 · August 14, 2024
"Why Economists Are Sounding the Alarm on Productivity"

Guy Gellatly explains why Canadian labour productivity declined in 12 of the 15 quarters before this episode and has fallen below pre-pandemic levels. Covers why roughly 90% of GDP-per-capita growth over four decades comes from labour productivity, what low business investment means for long-run living standards, and what AI might — or might not — change.

Economic Growth · Long-Run Aggregate Supply
SC
Eh Sayers · Ep. 18 · May 8, 2024
"Why Food Inflation Is Such a Hard Nut to Crack"

Taylor Mitchell of Statistics Canada's Prices Division explains the gap between official food CPI numbers and what Canadians feel at the checkout. Covers how grocery prices are collected, why the CPI basket construction matters, the role of supply chains and corporate margins, and why inflation can feel higher than the headline rate even when the data is accurate.

Inflation · CPI Measurement
SC
Eh Sayers · Ep. 4 · January 7, 2022
"Who Wins and Who Loses in the Gig Economy?"

Paul Glavin of McMaster University discusses with Statistics Canada how gig and platform work (Uber, Fiverr, TaskRabbit) reshapes labour force participation, job stability, and income distribution. Connects to the Unemployment chapter's discussion of non-standard employment, the Labour Force Survey's classification challenges, and why the headline unemployment rate can miss precarious work.

Growth & Unemployment · Labour Markets

How to use these podcasts in ECON 101. These are supplementary resources — nothing here is required listening. The most effective approach is to listen to an episode the same week you cover that topic in lecture. Active listening means pausing to ask yourself: Which model explains this? What shift is the host describing? Is this a movement along a curve or a shift of the curve? Translating the story back into the analytical framework is the real learning exercise.
