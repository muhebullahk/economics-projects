\section{Curriculum Development and Teaching Contributions}

\textbf{Course Development and Redesign}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Developed three complete online course packages — ECON 101, ECON 204, and ECON 220 — for initial asynchronous delivery at UNBC, including lecture slides, practice problem sets, tutorials, quizzes, and examinations (2024--25).
  \item Designed ECON 308 (International Economic Relations) and ECON 320 (Introduction to Mathematical Economics) as new course preparations for Winter 2025, each with full instructional and assessment materials.
  \item Substantially redesigned ECON 101 for in-person delivery in Fall 2025, introducing active-learning components, in-class discussion, and structured problem sessions alongside a revised assessment design.
  \item Redesigned ECON 308 comprehensively for Fall 2026 using Krugman, Obstfeld, and Melitz (12th edition), with original custom lecture slides featuring Canadian macroeconomic applications, TikZ-typeset diagrams, and a new pedagogical sequence integrating trade theory with open-economy macroeconomics.
  \item Developed ECON 300 (Labour Economics) for Winter 2026, using Benjamin et al.\ \textit{Labour Market Economics} (9th edition) with original quizzes, assignments, and a progressive midterm structure.
  \item Prepared ECON 311 (Behavioural Economics) as a new offering for Fall 2026.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Instructional Materials in Progress}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textit{Contemporary Economic Issues: A Canadian Perspective} — original seven-chapter LaTeX textbook for ECON 204 / INTS 211, addressing a gap in Canadian-focused resources for this course; developed for use in Winter 2027.
  \item \textit{Applied Econometrics} — thirteen-chapter course notes for ECON 621 (cross-listed senior undergraduate and graduate), covering causal inference, panel data, instrumental variables, difference-in-differences, matching, and regression discontinuity, with integrated R labs; developed for use in Winter 2027.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Technology and Pedagogical Innovation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Delivered courses using Moodle and Achieve learning management systems for assessments, assignment submission, and student communication.
  \item Incorporated real Canadian macroeconomic data (Statistics Canada, Bank of Canada, IMF) into lectures and assignments to connect theory with current evidence.
  \item Developed custom Excel and Python-based simulations for topics including the money multiplier, output gap analysis, and exchange rate adjustments.
  \item Produced structured digital lecture materials for hybrid and online course delivery during the transition to remote teaching.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Student Mentorship}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Maintained an open-door advising policy, meeting regularly with students on academic performance, graduate school preparation, and career planning.
  \item Supported students applying to doctoral programs; former students have been admitted to highly competitive graduate economics programs in Canada and internationally.
  \item Adapted course materials and provided supplementary guidance to students with varied mathematics and writing backgrounds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Interests}

Macroeconomics $\cdot$ Dynamic Stochastic General Equilibrium (DSGE) Modelling $\cdot$ Business Cycle Analysis $\cdot$ International Monetary Economics $\cdot$ Housing Market Economics $\cdot$ Labour Market Dynamics

My research uses structural macroeconomic modelling and applied microdata methods to examine business cycle fluctuations, housing investment, and labour market outcomes. Current projects employ Statistics Canada microdata accessed through the Research Data Centre (RDC) at UNBC. My research agenda complements my undergraduate teaching in macroeconomics, international economics, and labour economics.
