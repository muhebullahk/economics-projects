### LECTURE 1 — World Trade: An Overview
**Textbook:** Chapter 2  
**F25 primary file:** `Lecture 1 (Ch. 2).pptx` (31 slides) + `Lectures/Lecture 1/Lecture 1.tex` (Beamer)  
**F26 status:** ✅ Redesigned (`Slides/Lec1/Lec1.tex`, 38 slides)

#### Topics Covered
1. Who trades with whom (stylized facts, US top-15 partners)
2. Size and trade: the gravity model (simple and structural)
3. Impediments to trade: distance and borders
4. Two ages of globalization and their reversals
5. Composition of world trade: goods → manufacturing → services
6. Global value chains (GVCs): fragmentation, intermediate inputs
7. Trade policy levers and macro linkages

#### Models
- **Gravity model** (empirical and structural)

#### Key Equations
| Equation | Description |
|---|---|
| $T_{ij} = A\, Y_i^{\alpha} Y_j^{\beta} / D_{ij}^{\gamma} \cdot \exp\{z_{ij}'\delta\}$ | General gravity equation |
| $\ln T_{ij} = c + \alpha \ln Y_i + \beta \ln Y_j - \gamma \ln D_{ij} + z_{ij}'\delta + \varepsilon_{ij}$ | Log-linear gravity (OLS/PPML) |
| $X_{ij} = \frac{Y_i E_j}{Y^W} \left(\frac{\tau_{ij}}{\Pi_i P_j}\right)^{1-\sigma}$ | Structural (CES) gravity with multilateral resistance |
| $Y = C + I + G + (X - M)$ | Trade as component of aggregate demand [F26 addition] |

#### Figures
- Fig 2.1: Total US trade with top 15 partners, 2019
- Fig 2.2: European GDP share vs. US trade share
- Fig 2.3: Gravity model fit (distance vs. trade)
- Fig 2.4: Trade/GDP ratio over time (two waves of globalization)
- Fig 2.5: Composition of world trade (goods/services split)
- Fig 2.6: GVC participation indicators

#### Examples Used
- US–Canada, US–Mexico, US–China, US–Japan, US–Germany trade
- UK, France, Germany as top European partners
- BC lumber exports to the US vs. Asia [F26 addition]
- Canada–US \$700B bilateral relationship [F26 addition]

#### Learning Objectives
After this lecture students should be able to:
1. State the gravity model and interpret its parameters ($\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$).
2. Explain why GDP and distance predict bilateral trade.
3. Identify channels through which borders reduce trade beyond tariffs.
4. Summarize the two ages of globalization and their reversal mechanisms.
5. Distinguish inter-industry from intra-industry trade and goods from services trade.
6. Describe global value chains and their measurement challenges.
7. Connect trade openness to aggregate demand and business cycle dynamics. [F26]

### LECTURE 2 — Labor Productivity and Comparative Advantage (Ricardian Model)
**Textbook:** Chapter 3  
**F25 primary files:** `Lecture 2 (Ch.3).pptx` (81 slides, full); `Lecture 2 (Ch.3 Part 1).pptx` (28); `Lecture 3 (Ch.3 Part 2).pptx` (38)  
**F26 status:** ✅ Redesigned — `Slides/Ch3/Ch3.tex` (30 slides, 0 errors, 0 overflows)

#### Topics Covered
1. Concept of comparative advantage (Ricardo; opportunity cost)
2. Absolute vs. comparative advantage
3. A one-factor economy: PPF under constant returns
4. Autarky relative prices as unit labor cost ratios
5. Gains from trade: consumption beyond the PPF
6. Relative wages under free trade
7. Misconceptions: the pauper-labor argument
8. Empirical evidence (Dornbusch-Fischer-Samuelson continuum model)

#### Models
- **Ricardian model**: 2 countries (Home, Foreign), 2 goods (Wine/Cloth or Wheat/Cheese), 1 factor (labor)

#### Key Equations
| Equation | Description |
|---|---|
| $a_{LW},\, a_{LC}$ | Unit labor requirements (hours per unit output) |
| PPF: $a_{LC} Q_C + a_{LW} Q_W = L$ | Linear production frontier |
| $P_W^{autarky} / P_C^{autarky} = a_{LW}/a_{LC}$ | Autarky price = opportunity cost |
| Trade when: $a_{LW}/a_{LC} \neq a^*_{LW}/a^*_{LC}$ | Condition for comparative advantage |
| Relative wages: $w/w^* \in [a^*_{LW}/a_{LW},\, a^*_{LC}/a_{LC}]$ | Free-trade wage band |
| $(P_W/P_C)^{world}$ pins both wage ratio and specialization | |

#### Figures
- Linear PPF diagram (Ricardian)
- Relative supply (RS) and relative demand (RD) curves
- Gains from trade: PPF vs. consumption point under free trade
- Wage ratio and specialization diagram

#### Examples Used (F25)
- Home: $a_{LW}=2$, $a_{LC}=4$; Foreign: $a_{LW}=6$, $a_{LC}=3$; $L_H=L_F=240$
- World price $P_W/P_C = 1.2$ → Home specializes in Wine
- Quiz 1: Home $a_W=2$, $a_C=1$; Foreign $a_W=6$, $a_C=2$; $L=120$ each
- Three-country extension (PPS1 Q7): countries 1,2,3 with $a_{LW}=1,2,4$ and $a_{LC}=2,3,5$

#### Learning Objectives
1. Define comparative advantage using opportunity cost; distinguish from absolute advantage.
2. Derive and interpret a Ricardian PPF.
3. Explain why autarky relative prices equal unit labor cost ratios under perfect competition.
4. Identify the world relative price range consistent with complete specialization.
5. Compute production bundles, trade patterns, and real wages under free trade.
6. Refute the pauper-labor argument using Ricardian logic.
7. Describe empirical patterns consistent with and inconsistent with the Ricardian model.

### LECTURE 3 — Specific Factors and Income Distribution
**Textbook:** Chapter 4  
**F25 primary files:** `Lecture 5 (Ch.4).pptx` (71 slides, full); `Lecture 5 (Ch.4 Part 1).pptx` (32); `Lecture 7 (Ch.4 Part 2).pptx` (38); `Exercise 1.tex` (in-class)  
**F26 status:** ✅ Redesigned — `Slides/Ch4/Ch4.tex` (27 slides, 0 errors, 0 overflows)

#### Topics Covered
1. Model setup: two goods (Manufactures M, Food F), three factors (K specific to M, T specific to F, L mobile)
2. Production functions and marginal products
3. Labor market equilibrium: wage equalization via VMPL
4. Effect of a price change on wages and factor returns
5. Income distribution effects of trade: winners and losers
6. Political economy of trade (short-run vs. long-run)
7. The immigration analogy
8. Two-country extension: comparative advantage and trade patterns

#### Models
- **Specific Factors model**: 2 goods, 3 factors (L mobile, K and T sector-specific)

#### Key Equations
| Equation | Description |
|---|---|
| $Q_M = Q_M(K, L_M)$, $Q_F = Q_F(T, L_F)$ | Production functions |
| $L_M + L_F = \bar{L}$ | Labor market clearing |
| $P_M \cdot MPL_M = P_F \cdot MPL_F = w$ | Wage equalization condition |
| $MPL_M = \partial Q_M/\partial L_M$ | Marginal product (decreasing) |
| $r_K = P_M \cdot MPK_M$; $r_T = P_F \cdot MPT_F$ | Factor returns |
| $OC_M = MPL_F / MPL_M$ | Opportunity cost = slope of PPF |
| Real wage ambiguity: $\uparrow w/P_M < \uparrow P_M/P_M$; $\uparrow w/P_F$ | Ambiguous labor welfare effect |

#### Figures
- VMPL diagram: $w_M(L_M)$ and $w_F(L_F)$ crossing; shift after price change
- PPF (smooth, concave) for specific-factors model
- Income distribution: gains/losses by factor after price change
- TikZ diagram in `Exercise 1.tex`: $w_M = 12/\sqrt{L_M}$ and $w_F = 4/\sqrt{100-L_M}$

#### Examples Used (F25)
- Home: $K=144$, $T=64$, $L=100$; $Q_M = K^{1/2}L_M^{1/2}$, $Q_F = T^{1/2}L_F^{1/2}$
- $P_M=2$: equilibrium $L_M=90$, $w≈1.265$; after $P_M=3$: $L_M≈95.2$, $w≈1.845$
- Foreign reversed: $K^*=64$, $T^*=144$ → Home exports M, Foreign exports F
- Quiz 2: $K=64$, $T=100$, $L=90$; $P_M=2→3$, $P_F=1$
- Final Exam Review: $Q_M=5K^{1/2}L_M^{1/2}$, $Q_F=4T^{1/2}L_F^{1/2}$; $L=200$, $K=T=100$

#### Learning Objectives
1. Set up the specific-factors model and identify which factors are mobile and which are specific.
2. Derive MPL in each sector and solve for the labor market equilibrium wage.
3. Analyze the effect of a price change ($\uparrow P_M$) on wages, factor returns, and outputs.
4. Explain why labor's real wage effect is ambiguous while specific-factor returns are unambiguous.
5. Identify winners and losers from trade within the specific-factors framework.
6. Derive a country's comparative advantage from factor endowments in the short run.
7. Discuss why political opposition to trade is predictable from this model.

### LECTURE 4 — Resources and Trade: The Heckscher-Ohlin Model

#### Topics Covered

4. The Heckscher-Ohlin theorem
5. Factor price equalization (FPE) theorem
6. Stolper-Samuelson theorem
7. Rybczynski theorem
8. Empirical tests: the Leontief Paradox
9. Trade and inequality

#### Models
- **Heckscher-Ohlin model**: 2 goods (Cloth C, Food F or Computers, Textiles), 2 factors (K, L), 2 countries

#### Key Equations
| Equation | Description |
|---|---|
| $(K/L)_H > (K/L)_F$ | Definition of capital abundance |
| $(K/L)_C > (K/L)_F$ | Definition of capital intensity of good C |
| $P_X = a_{LX}w + a_{KX}r$; $P_Y = a_{LY}w + a_{KY}r$ | Zero-profit conditions |
| H-O: $\uparrow (K/L)_H \Rightarrow$ Home exports capital-intensive good | HO theorem |
| FPE: Free trade equalizes $w$ and $r$ across countries | Factor price equalization |
| SS: $\uparrow P_C \Rightarrow \uparrow w$ (real), $\downarrow r$ (real) if C is labor-intensive | Stolper-Samuelson |
| Rybczynski: $\uparrow L \Rightarrow \uparrow Q_C$, $\downarrow Q_K$ (at fixed prices) | Rybczynski theorem |
| $K/L_H = 0.6/(0.4) \cdot (w/r)$ (Cobb-Douglas cost minimization) | Factor demand |

#### Figures
- Factor box (Edgeworth box): allocation of K and L across sectors
- Bowed-out PPF: capital and labour endowments determine shape
- Relative supply curve shifts with factor endowment changes
- Rybczynski theorem: biased PPF expansion
- Leontief Paradox: US capital/labor ratio in exports vs. imports (1947 data)

#### Examples Used (F25)
- Canada (Home: $K=600$, $L=300$) vs. Mexico (Foreign: $K=300$, $L=600$)
- $Q_C = K_C^{0.6}L_C^{0.4}$; $Q_T = K_T^{0.3}L_T^{0.7}$; Computers capital-intensive, Textiles labour-intensive
- Tutorial 1: autarky $w/r$ → equilibrium $K/L$ ratios → comparative advantage
- Quiz 3: $K_H=300$, $L_H=300$; $K_F=100$, $L_F=300$; Computers ($a_K=6$, $a_L=3$) vs. Textiles ($a_K=2$, $a_L=4$)
- Leontief Paradox discussed qualitatively

#### Learning Objectives
1. Compute factor abundance ratios and classify countries as capital- or labour-abundant.
2. Determine factor intensity of goods using $K/L$ ratios.
3. State and apply the Heckscher-Ohlin theorem to predict trade patterns.
4. Explain factor price equalization and its empirical limitations.
5. Apply the Stolper-Samuelson theorem to identify winners and losers from trade.
6. Apply the Rybczynski theorem to predict output effects of factor accumulation.
7. Describe the Leontief Paradox and evaluate proposed explanations.
8. Discuss the connection between HO trade and income inequality.

| Version | Slides | Notes |

| F26 target | 35–38 | Tighten; add Canadian factor endowment data |

### LECTURE 5 — The Standard Trade Model
**Textbook:** Chapter 6  
**F25 primary file:** `Lectures on Ch.6.pptx` (52 slides); `Exercise 2.tex`, `Quiz 4.tex`  
**F26 status:** 🔄 Pending redesign

#### Topics Covered
1. Standard model as general case (Ricardian, SF, HO as special cases)
2. Production possibilities and relative supply
3. Indifference curves and relative demand
4. World equilibrium: RS = RD
5. Terms of trade: definition and welfare effects
6. Growth and trade: export-biased vs. import-biased growth
7. Immiserizing growth and the Prebisch-Singer hypothesis
8. Effects of international transfers
9. International borrowing and lending (brief)
10. Tariffs and export subsidies in the Standard Model

#### Models
- **Standard Trade Model**: smooth bowed-out PPF, homothetic preferences, terms of trade as welfare lever

#### Key Equations
| Equation | Description |
|---|---|
| $RS = Q_X/Q_Y = f(P_X/P_Y)$ | Relative supply (upward-sloping) |
| $RD = D_X/D_Y = g(P_X/P_Y)$ | Relative demand (downward-sloping) |
| RS = RD → $(P_X/P_Y)^W$ | World equilibrium terms of trade |
| $ToT = P_{exports}/P_{imports}$ | Terms of trade definition |
| $\uparrow ToT \Rightarrow$ outward pivot of trade line $\Rightarrow$ ↑ welfare | Welfare effect of ToT improvement |
| Isovalue line: $P_X Q_X + P_Y Q_Y = V$ (slope $= -P_X/P_Y$) | Income/value maximization |
| Export-biased growth → ↑RS → ↓ToT | Immiserizing growth condition |
| $F^2 + M^2 = 100$ (or $A^2 + 4E^2 = 1600$) | Example PPF equation |

#### Figures
- PPF with isovalue lines: production point maximizes value of output
- Indifference curves: consumption at tangency with isovalue line
- RS/RD diagram: world price determination
- Growth: two types of PPF expansion and their RS/RD effects
- International transfers: donor loses, recipient gains (unless immiserizing)
